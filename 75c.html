Notes (Key points from the speech)

What is the Haber process?
  • The Haber process is the industrial production of ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen.
  • Ammonia is important because it is used to make nitrogen-based fertilizers.
  • Fertilizers help produce enough food for the world's population.
Sources of reactants
  • Nitrogen is taken from the air because air is about 78% nitrogen.
  • Hydrogen is produced from hydrocarbons such as methane.
Reaction conditions
  • The Haber process uses a temperature of 450°C.
  • The pressure used is about 200 atmospheres.
  • An iron catalyst is used to increase the rate of reaction.
Reaction features
  • The Haber process is an exothermic reaction.
  • The reaction is reversible, meaning ammonia can break down back into nitrogen and hydrogen.
  • Not all reactants are converted into ammonia in one pass.
How the process works
  • Nitrogen and hydrogen gases are mixed in the reaction vessel.
  • The gases pass over the iron catalyst under high temperature and pressure.
  • Some ammonia forms while some nitrogen and hydrogen remain unreacted.
Separating ammonia
  • The gas mixture is cooled in a condenser.
  • Ammonia has a lower boiling point, so it condenses into a liquid.
  • Nitrogen and hydrogen stay as gases and are recycled back into the system.
Why 450°C is used
  • A lower temperature would give a higher percentage yield because the reaction is exothermic.
  • A higher temperature increases the rate of reaction.
  • 450°C is a compromise between yield, speed, and cost.
Why 200 atmospheres is used
  • High pressure increases the percentage yield of ammonia.
  • High pressure also increases the rate of reaction by causing more frequent collisions.
  • Very high pressures are expensive and dangerous, so 200 atmospheres is chosen as a practical compromise.
