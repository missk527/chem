Le Chatelier's Principle

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

Equilibrium basics
  • Equilibrium in a reversible reaction is the balance between reactants and products.
  • “Lies to the left” = more reactants; “lies to the right” = more products.
  • Le Chatelier’s principle: if conditions change, the equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
Temperature
  • Example: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (ΔH = −92 kJ·mol⁻¹) → forward reaction is exothermic.
  • Lowering temperature → shifts in the exothermic direction (here, to the right, making more NH₃).
  • Raising temperature → shifts in the endothermic direction (here, to the left, making more N₂ and H₂).
Pressure (for gases)
  • Increasing pressure → shifts to the side with fewer gas molecules.
  • In N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, the left has 4 molecules, the right has 2 molecules.
  • So higher pressure shifts to the right (toward NH₃). Lower pressure shifts to the left.
Concentration
  • Increasing the concentration of a reactant drives equilibrium to the opposite side to use it up.
  • Example: adding N₂ shifts to the right, producing more NH₃.
