Life Cycle Assessments

Notes

  • A life cycle assessment (LCA) studies every stage in a product’s life to judge its environmental impact.
  • The four main stages are raw material extraction and processing, manufacturing and packaging, product use, and disposal.
  • Extracting raw materials can directly damage habitats, such as forests or land disturbed by mining.
  • Processing raw materials often uses large amounts of energy and may release pollutants.
  • Manufacturing and packaging can cause environmental harm through energy use, pollution, and waste products.
  • Some waste can be reused as raw material, but much of it must still be disposed of.
  • When judging the use stage, we consider both the damage caused during use and how long the product lasts.
  • A reusable product usually has a different impact from a single-use product.
  • Disposal methods also matter because landfill uses land and may allow chemicals to leak into ecosystems.
  • Burning waste can release pollutants into the air.
  • Transporting waste to landfill or an incinerator also requires energy.
  • Plastic bags are made from crude oil, a finite resource, and the oil must be separated by fractional distillation.
  • Paper bags are made from wood, so producing them can reduce habitats for plants and animals.
  • Making plastic bags involves cracking hydrocarbons to form alkenes and then polymerising them into plastics.
  • Making paper bags involves pulping and soaking timber, which can use a lot of energy and create waste.
  • Plastic bags are often reused several times, while paper bags are often single-use.
  • Plastic bags are not biodegradable, so they can remain in landfill or harm wildlife.
  • Paper bags are biodegradable and non-toxic, so they are usually better at the disposal stage.
  • Life cycle assessments are limited because it is hard to collect and quantify every step in a product’s life.
  • They are also difficult to judge fairly because different kinds of environmental harm are hard to compare, and results can be manipulated.
