Acids and Bases: Neutralisation and Soluble Salts

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

1) Neutralisation reactions
  • Acids react with different types of bases in neutralisation reactions.
  • The bases in this topic include metal oxides, metal hydroxides, and metal carbonates.
  • These reactions are useful because they can produce salts.
2) Acids with metal oxides and metal hydroxides
  • When an acid reacts with a metal oxide or a metal hydroxide, the products are always salt + water.
  • The positive metal ion from the base combines with the negative ion from the acid to make the salt.
  • The hydrogen from the acid combines with oxygen or hydroxide to form water.
3) Examples of salts formed
  • Hydrochloric acid reacting with sodium oxide forms sodium chloride and water.
  • Sulfuric acid reacting with potassium hydroxide forms potassium sulfate and water.
  • When writing equations, the salt formula must be correct and the whole equation must be balanced.
4) Acids with metal carbonates
  • When an acid reacts with a metal carbonate, the products are salt + water + carbon dioxide.
  • For example, nitric acid reacting with calcium carbonate forms calcium nitrate, water, and carbon dioxide.
  • Because calcium is 2+ and nitrate is 1−, two nitrate ions are needed to make calcium nitrate.
5) Making soluble salts
  • A soluble salt can be made by reacting an acid with an insoluble base.
  • Insoluble bases include many metal oxides, metal hydroxides, and metal carbonates.
  • The base is added in excess to make sure all the acid has been neutralised.
6) Practical method
  • First, place dilute acid in a beaker and gently warm it.
  • Add the insoluble base a little at a time until some solid no longer disappears.
  • This leftover solid shows that the base is in excess and the acid has fully reacted.
7) Filtering and crystallising
  • Filter the mixture to remove the excess insoluble base.
  • The filtrate contains the soluble salt dissolved in water.
  • Gently heat the solution to evaporate some water, then cool it so crystals form.
8) Drying the crystals
  • Filter the crystals from the remaining solution.
  • Dry the crystals by dabbing them with filter paper or leaving them somewhere warm.
  • A water bath or electric heater is safer than strong heating because overheating can damage the salt.
