Notes (Key points from the speech)

What is chemical analysis?
  • Chemical analysis (analytical chemistry) uses instruments and methods to separate, identify, and quantify substances.
  • Examples of techniques include paper chromatography (separating pigments), filtration and crystallisation (isolating solids), and distillation (separating liquids).
  • Analytical chemistry also includes chemical tests, such as tests for common gases.
Pure substances
  • A pure substance contains only one compound or one element.
  • Examples: pure water (only H2O molecules) and pure sodium chloride (NaCl).
  • If substances are mixed (e.g., salt + water), the result is a mixture and is not pure.
Melting and boiling points
  • Only pure substances melt and boil at specific temperatures.
  • Example data: water boils at 100°C; sodium chloride melts at 801°C.
  • We can use melting/boiling points to identify unknown substances by comparing results with reference data.
Physical tests vs chemical tests
  • Measuring melting/boiling point is a physical test (testing physical properties).
  • Reacting a substance with another chemical to identify it is a chemical test.
Impure substances
  • Impure substances (mixtures) do not have a single melting/boiling point.
  • They melt and boil over a range of temperatures that depends on mixture composition.
  • In general, impurities lower the melting point and raise the boiling point.
  • Example: salt water melts at about -2°C (vs 0°C) and boils at about 100.5°C (vs 100°C).
Formulations
  • A formulation is a mixture made using a specific formula with precise amounts of each component.
  • Formulations are designed to have a particular function (like a recipe for a product).
  • Examples: fuels, cleaning agents, paints, medicines, alloys, fertilisers, and food and drink.
  • Key feature: components are always present in the same proportions, and each component can provide a different property.
  • Example: green paint needs a pigment for colour and other components to help it stick and behave correctly.
Take-home message
  • Purity affects melting/boiling behaviour and helps with identification.
  • Formulations are controlled mixtures with fixed proportions designed for a specific use.
