40C. Electrolysis of Aluminium Oxide

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

0:00 - 0:45 | Extracting Metals from Oxides
  • Many metals are found naturally as metal oxides, such as aluminium oxide and copper oxide.
  • To obtain the pure metal, the oxygen must be removed from the metal oxide.
  • Removing oxygen from a compound is called reduction.
  • Carbon can reduce some metal oxides by displacing the metal and forming carbon dioxide.
0:45 - 1:24 | Why Electrolysis Is Needed
  • Reduction with carbon only works for metals that are less reactive than carbon, such as zinc, iron, and copper.
  • More reactive metals cannot be extracted using carbon, so electrolysis is required instead.
  • Electrolysis separates ionic compounds into their elements using an electric current.
  • Aluminium oxide can be electrolysed to produce aluminium and oxygen.
1:24 - 2:27 | Preparing Molten Aluminium Oxide
  • Electrolysis only works when ions are free to move.
  • Solid aluminium oxide cannot be electrolysed because its ions are fixed in place.
  • Aluminium oxide is purified from bauxite before electrolysis.
  • Cryolite is added to lower the melting point, reducing the amount of energy needed.
2:27 - 3:11 | Setting Up the Electrolysis Cell
  • The molten aluminium oxide mixture acts as the electrolyte.
  • Carbon electrodes are used because they conduct electricity.
  • The positive electrode is called the anode, and the negative electrode is called the cathode.
  • A power source and wires complete the circuit so electrons can flow.
3:11 - 4:05 | Ion Movement and Products
  • Molten aluminium oxide contains aluminium ions, Al³⁺, and oxide ions, O²⁻.
  • Oxide ions move to the positive anode, lose electrons, and form oxygen gas.
  • Aluminium ions move to the negative cathode, gain electrons, and form aluminium atoms.
  • Molten aluminium collects at the bottom of the electrolysis cell.
4:13 - 5:41 | Half Equations and Redox
  • At the anode, oxide ions lose electrons: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻.
  • At the cathode, aluminium ions gain electrons: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al.
  • Oxidation is loss of electrons, and reduction is gain of electrons.
  • The overall equation is 2Al₂O₃(l) → 4Al(l) + 3O₂(g).
