1) Overview: why we test gases

In the lab, gases are often identified using simple qualitative tests. This video focuses on four common gases—chlorine (Cl₂), oxygen (O₂), hydrogen (H₂), and carbon dioxide (CO₂)—and the observations you should record for each.

2) Chlorine test: damp blue litmus paper

Place damp blue litmus paper into the test tube of the gas. If chlorine is present, the paper is bleached white. It may briefly turn red first because chlorine dissolves in the water on the paper to form acidic solution, then it quickly turns white due to bleaching.

3) Safety note for chlorine

Chlorine is toxic. In real experiments, use a fume cupboard and appropriate PPE (for example, eye protection and a mask/respirator as required by local rules).

4) Oxygen test: relights a glowing splint

Insert a glowing splint (no flame, glowing ember) into the gas. If the gas is oxygen, the splint relights because oxygen supports combustion.

5) Hydrogen test: squeaky pop with a lit splint

Bring a lit splint to the mouth of the test tube. If hydrogen is present, it burns rapidly with oxygen from the air and makes a characteristic “squeaky pop” as water forms.

6) Carbon dioxide test: turns limewater cloudy

Bubble the gas through limewater (aqueous calcium hydroxide). Carbon dioxide reacts to form calcium carbonate (a white solid) and water, turning the solution cloudy/milky.

7) Quick recap (exam-friendly)
  • Chlorine: damp blue litmus → red (briefly) → white
  • Oxygen: relights a glowing splint
  • Hydrogen: lit splint gives squeaky pop
  • Carbon dioxide: limewater turns cloudy
