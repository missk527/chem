Hydrocarbons: Alkanes

Notes (Key Points)

  • Organic chemistry studies compounds that contain carbon.
  • Carbon forms four strong covalent bonds, often with hydrogen or other carbon atoms.
  • Hydrocarbons = compounds made of carbon and hydrogen only.
  • Butane (C4H10) is a hydrocarbon; butanol is not (contains oxygen).
  • Alkanes are the simplest hydrocarbons.
  • First four alkanes: methane (CH4), ethane (C2H6), propane (C3H8), butane (C4H10).
  • They form a homologous series: each increases by 1 carbon and 2 hydrogens.
  • General formula of alkanes: CnH2n+2.
  • Example: n=3 → C3H8 (propane), n=8 → C8H18 (octane).
  • Alkanes are saturated: all carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds.
  • If a double bond is added, the molecule becomes an alkene, not an alkane.
