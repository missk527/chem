Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

1) What is corrosion?
  • Corrosion is the process where metals are slowly broken down by reacting with substances in their environment.
  • A common example is rusting in iron.
2) Rusting process
  • Rusting occurs when iron reacts with oxygen and water.
  • The product formed is hydrated iron oxide (rust).
3) Redox reactions
  • Iron loses electrons → oxidation.
  • Oxygen gains electrons → reduction.
  • Rusting is a redox reaction.
4) Conditions for rusting
  • Both oxygen and water are required.
  • If either is missing, rusting does not occur.
5) Experimental evidence
  • No oxygen → no rust.
  • No water → no rust.
  • Both present → rust forms.
6) Surface corrosion
  • Only the surface of a metal corrodes.
  • In iron, rust flakes off exposing new metal, allowing further corrosion.
7) Aluminium corrosion
  • Aluminium forms a protective oxide layer.
  • This prevents further corrosion.
8) Preventing rusting
  • Barrier methods: painting, oiling/greasing, electroplating.
  • Sacrificial methods: using a more reactive metal like zinc or aluminium.
  • Galvanising combines both methods.
