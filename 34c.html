Notes (Key points rewritten from the speech)

What is an acid?
  • An acid forms an aqueous solution with a pH below 7.
  • Acids release hydrogen ions (H+) when they ionise in water.
  • A hydrogen ion is a hydrogen atom that has lost its electron, so it is essentially a proton.
Strong acids
  • Strong acids ionise completely in aqueous solution.
  • This means all acid particles dissociate to release hydrogen ions.
  • Examples include hydrochloric acid, nitric acid, and sulfuric acid.
Weak acids
  • Weak acids only partially ionise in water.
  • Only a small proportion of weak acid particles dissociate to release hydrogen ions.
  • Examples include ethanoic acid, citric acid, and carbonic acid.
  • The ionisation of weak acids is reversible, so an equilibrium forms between undissociated acid and dissociated ions.
  • For weak acids, the equilibrium lies mostly to the left, meaning there are more undissociated acid molecules than ions.
Strength is not the same as concentration
  • Acid strength describes the proportion of acid molecules that ionise in water.
  • Concentration describes how much acid is dissolved in a certain volume of solution.
  • A strong acid can be either concentrated or dilute.
  • A weak acid can also be either concentrated or dilute.
pH and hydrogen ion concentration
  • pH measures the concentration of hydrogen ions in a solution.
  • A higher hydrogen ion concentration gives a lower pH.
  • Each decrease of 1 pH unit means the hydrogen ion concentration increases by a factor of 10.
  • Moving from pH 5 to pH 3 means the hydrogen ion concentration becomes 100 times higher.
  • Moving from pH 8 to pH 11 means the hydrogen ion concentration becomes 1000 times lower.
Putting the ideas together
  • At the same concentration, a strong acid has a lower pH than a weak acid because it releases more hydrogen ions.
  • A weak acid needs to be very concentrated to produce a very low pH, because only a small proportion of its particles ionise.
  • The pH of a solution depends on the hydrogen ion concentration, not simply on the name or amount of acid present.
