42C Exothermic & Endothermic Reactions

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

0:00 - 1:00 | Energy in Chemical Reactions
  • Different chemicals store different amounts of energy in their bonds.
  • Reactants and products can have different total energy levels.
  • Reaction profiles show energy changes during reactions.
  • The y-axis shows total energy and the x-axis shows reaction progress.
1:00 - 2:20 | Exothermic Reactions
  • Exothermic reactions release energy to the surroundings.
  • Products have less energy than reactants.
  • Heat is usually transferred to the surroundings.
  • Temperature increases during exothermic reactions.
  • Combustion and neutralisation are common exothermic reactions.
2:20 - 3:15 | Endothermic Reactions
  • Endothermic reactions absorb energy from the surroundings.
  • Products have more energy than reactants.
  • Breaking down calcium carbonate requires heat.
  • Energy absorbed is shown on reaction profiles.
3:15 - 4:10 | Activation Energy
  • Activation energy is the minimum energy needed for particles to react.
  • Higher activation energy means more energy is needed to start reactions.
  • Reaction profiles use curves to show activation energy.
  • The highest point on the curve represents activation energy.
4:10 - 4:54 | Drawing Reaction Profiles
  • Both exothermic and endothermic reactions need activation energy.
  • Higher curves show higher activation energy.
  • Lower curves show lower activation energy.
  • Specific chemicals can be written on reaction profile diagrams.
