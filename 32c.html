Percentage Yield

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

1) Yield in chemistry
  • The amount of product formed in a chemical reaction is called the yield.
  • Yield can be measured in grams or moles.
  • Chemists compare the amount expected with the amount actually obtained.
2) Actual yield and theoretical yield
  • Actual yield is the amount of product you really collect from the experiment.
  • Theoretical yield is the amount you calculate should be made if the reaction works perfectly.
  • Example: 2 g of hydrogen reacting with 16 g of oxygen should produce 18 g of water, so 18 g is the theoretical yield.
3) Why actual yield is often lower
  • In real experiments, the actual yield is often less than the theoretical yield.
  • For example, instead of 18 g of water, you might collect only 15 g.
  • This difference happens because real reactions and practical methods are not perfect.
4) Reason 1: not all reactants react
  • Some reactants may remain unreacted at the end.
  • This can happen if the reaction is slow and has not had enough time to finish.
  • It can also happen in a reversible reaction because equilibrium prevents complete conversion.
5) Reason 2: side reactions
  • Reactants may produce an unexpected product instead of the desired one.
  • These extra reactions are called side reactions.
  • Example: some nitrogen might react with oxygen in the air to form nitrogen dioxide instead of ammonia.
6) Reason 3: loss of product
  • Some product may be lost during transfer, filtering, scraping, or collection.
  • Gases can escape into the air.
  • Liquids and solids can be left behind in beakers, on filter paper, or stuck to apparatus.
7) Percentage yield
  • Percentage yield = actual yield ÷ theoretical yield × 100.
  • A percentage yield can range from 0% to 100%.
  • Example: 15 g ÷ 18 g × 100 = 83.3%, meaning 83.3% of the expected product was obtained.
