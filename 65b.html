Flame Emission Spectroscopy — Identifying Metal Ions

Notes (rewritten in English from the given speech)

What flame emission spectroscopy is
  • Flame emission spectroscopy, also called flame photometry, is used to identify metal ions.
  • When metal ions are heated, they emit light at specific wavelengths.
  • These wavelengths produce characteristic colours and a unique line spectrum for each metal ion.

How it compares with the flame test
  • A simple flame test heats a sample in a Bunsen burner and observes the flame colour.
  • This is useful for a quick test, but it can be difficult when different metals produce similar colours.
  • It is also less reliable if the sample contains more than one metal ion, because the colours mix together.
  • Flame emission spectroscopy solves this by measuring the exact wavelengths of light with a spectroscope.
Unique line spectra
  • Each metal ion produces its own pattern of spectral lines.
  • This pattern is called a line spectrum.
  • By comparing an unknown sample with standard spectra from known metal ions, scientists can identify which ions are present.
  • If a sample contains several ions, the final spectrum shows the combined lines from all of them.
Finding concentration
  • The brightness or intensity of a spectral line shows how much of that ion is present.
  • Brighter lines mean a higher concentration of that metal ion.
  • This means flame emission spectroscopy can identify ions and measure their concentrations at the same time.
Why instrumental methods are better
  • Flame emission spectroscopy is an instrumental method, while a flame test is a manual test.
  • Instrumental methods are usually more accurate, more sensitive, and much faster.
  • They can detect very small amounts of a substance and are often automated.
  • Although they use expensive equipment, they usually produce better results.
