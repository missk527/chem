39C. Electrolysis of Molten Lead Bromide

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

0:00 - 0:14 | What Electrolysis Does
  • Electrolysis means splitting substances using electricity.
  • It can separate the elements in ionic compounds.
  • This video focuses on insoluble compounds such as lead bromide.
0:14 - 1:12 | Electrolytes and Moving Ions
  • An electrolyte is a liquid or solution containing an ionic compound.
  • The ions in an electrolyte must be free to move.
  • Soluble compounds can be dissolved in water to make an aqueous electrolyte.
  • Insoluble compounds must be melted to form a molten liquid.
  • Molten lead bromide contains Pb²⁺ ions and Br⁻ ions that can move freely.
1:12 - 1:42 | Electrodes and Circuit
  • Electrodes are solid conductors, usually made from metal or carbon.
  • The positive electrode is called the anode.
  • The negative electrode is called the cathode.
  • Wires allow electrons to flow around the circuit.
  • A battery or power supply drives the movement of electrons.
1:42 - 2:35 | Separating Lead Bromide
  • Passing current through molten lead bromide separates lead and bromine.
  • Negative bromide ions move to the positive anode.
  • Positive lead ions move to the negative cathode.
  • Bromide ions are discharged to form bromine gas.
  • Lead ions are discharged to form pure molten lead.
  • The molten lead collects at the bottom of the container.
2:35 - 3:04 | Oxidation and Reduction
  • At the anode, bromide ions lose electrons and are oxidised.
  • Bromide ions form bromine molecules.
  • At the cathode, lead ions gain electrons and are reduced.
  • Lead ions become neutral lead atoms.
  • Half equations can show the electron transfer at each electrode.
3:04 - 3:43 | Overall Electron Transfer
  • Electrons are taken from bromide ions at the anode.
  • The battery moves these electrons through the wire to the cathode.
  • Lead ions receive electrons at the cathode and become pure lead.
  • Electrolysis uses electricity to turn ions back into their elemental forms.
  • Overall, electrons are transferred from negative ions to positive ions.
