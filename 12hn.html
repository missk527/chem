Group 7 (Halogens) & Group 0 (Noble Gases) Properties · Trends · Reactions

Notes (Key points rewritten from the speech)

Overview
  • Group 7 — Halogens: fluorine (F), chlorine (Cl), bromine (Br), iodine (I). (Astatine/tennessine are usually ignored in basic courses.)
  • Group 0 — Noble gases: He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn. Colorless, inert gases with full outer shells.
Halogens: physical properties & hazards
  • Fluorine: poisonous, pale yellow gas; very reactive.
  • Chlorine: poisonous green gas; less reactive than fluorine.
  • Bromine: red‑brown volatile liquid; poisonous.
  • Iodine: dark gray solid; forms purple vapors; also used as an antiseptic.
Halogens: bonding & structure
  • Exist as diatomic molecules (X2) via covalent bonds (shared electron pairs).
  • Form covalent compounds with non‑metals (e.g., hydrogen fluoride HF, carbon tetrachloride CCl4); these are simple molecular structures.
  • Form ionic bonds with metals by gaining one electron → halide ions (F⁻, Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻). Naming ends in “‑ide”: fluoride, chloride, bromide, iodide.
Halogens: periodic trends (down the group)
  • Melting & boiling points: increase down the group.
  • Reactivity: decreases down the group because the outer shell is farther from the nucleus, so it attracts an extra electron less strongly.
  • Thus, fluorine is most reactive, iodine least reactive.
Halogen displacement reactions
  • A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halogen from its compound in solution.
  • Example: Cl2 + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br2 (chlorine displaces bromide from potassium bromide).
  • General rule: F2, Cl2, Br2 can all displace I2 (since iodine is least reactive).
Noble gases: properties & trend
  • Colorless gases; full outer shells → inert (very unreactive).
  • Exist as single atoms (monatomic), not molecules.
  • Non‑flammable.
  • Boiling points increase down the group.
