38C. Redox Reactions

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

0:00 - 1:10 | Oxidation and Reduction
  • Redox reactions involve oxidation and reduction happening at the same time.
  • Oxidation can mean gaining oxygen, while reduction can mean losing oxygen.
  • Oxidation can also mean losing electrons.
  • Reduction can also mean gaining electrons.
  • The mnemonic OIL RIG helps: Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain.
1:10 - 2:02 | Redox Happens Together
  • Oxidation and reduction usually occur together because electrons are transferred.
  • If one substance loses electrons, another substance must gain them.
  • Magnesium is oxidized when it loses two electrons to form Mg²⁺.
  • Hydrogen ions are reduced when they gain electrons to form hydrogen gas.
  • Redox means reduction-oxidation.
2:02 - 3:07 | Displacement and Ionic Equations
  • Displacement reactions happen when a more reactive metal replaces a less reactive metal.
  • Calcium can displace iron because calcium is more reactive than iron.
  • In calcium plus iron sulfate, calcium sulfate forms and iron is produced as a solid.
  • Ionic equations show only the particles that actually change during the reaction.
  • Sulfate ions are spectator ions because they do not change or exchange electrons.
3:07 - 4:07 | Half Equations
  • Half equations show electron loss or electron gain for one element at a time.
  • Calcium is oxidized: Ca → Ca²⁺ + 2e⁻.
  • Iron(II) ions are reduced: Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Fe.
  • Electrons go on the product side when they are lost.
  • Electrons go on the reactant side when they are gained.
4:07 - 4:29 | Checking Charges
  • In half equations, the total charge must be the same on both sides.
  • For Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Fe, the left side has an overall charge of zero.
  • The right side is neutral iron, so it also has an overall charge of zero.
  • If the charges do not balance, the electrons may be on the wrong side.
  • Balancing charges helps confirm that the half equation is correct.
