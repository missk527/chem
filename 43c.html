43C. Bond Energies

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

0:00 - 0:58 | Bond Energies Basics
  • Bond energy is the energy needed to break one mole of covalent bonds.
  • Breaking bonds requires energy and is endothermic.
  • Forming bonds releases energy and is exothermic.
  • One mole contains 6.02 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro’s constant).
0:58 - 1:39 | Hydrogen and Chlorine Reaction
  • Hydrogen reacts with chlorine to form hydrogen chloride.
  • The H–H bond and Cl–Cl bond must be broken first.
  • Breaking bonds absorbs energy.
  • Two H–Cl bonds are formed and release energy.
1:39 - 2:36 | Calculating Energy Change
  • Total energy change compares bond breaking and bond forming.
  • Formula: Energy to break bonds − Energy released forming bonds.
  • Negative values mean exothermic reactions.
  • Positive values mean endothermic reactions.
2:36 - 2:49 | Exothermic Result
  • The hydrogen chloride reaction gives −184 kJ/mol.
  • The reaction releases energy to the surroundings.
  • This confirms the reaction is exothermic.
2:49 - 3:58 | Nitrogen and Hydrogen Example
  • Nitrogen reacts with hydrogen to form ammonia.
  • One nitrogen triple bond and three hydrogen bonds are broken.
  • Six nitrogen-hydrogen bonds are formed.
  • The final energy change is −16 kJ/mol.
  • This reaction is also exothermic.
