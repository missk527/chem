Notes (Key points from the speech)

What metal reactivity means
  • Metals have outer-shell electrons that they tend to lose during reactions.
  • When a metal loses electrons, it forms a positive ion.
  • The reactivity of a metal means how easily it forms positive ions.
  • Metals that form positive ions most easily are the most reactive.
The reactivity series
  • The reactivity series arranges metals in order of reactivity.
  • Carbon and hydrogen are not metals, but they are often included as useful reference points.
  • Group 1 metals are among the most reactive metals.
  • Group 2 metals are generally less reactive than Group 1 metals.
  • Transition metals are generally less reactive than Group 1 and Group 2 metals.
Reactions with acids
  • When a metal reacts with an acid, it forms a salt and hydrogen gas.
  • General word equation: metal + acid → salt + hydrogen.
  • Example: potassium + hydrochloric acid → potassium chloride + hydrogen.
  • Very reactive metals, such as potassium, may react explosively, catch fire, or move around quickly as hydrogen is produced.
  • Less reactive metals react more slowly and produce fewer bubbles.
  • Magnesium reacts with acid and produces lots of bubbles as the metal disappears.
  • Zinc and iron produce fewer bubbles than magnesium.
  • Copper usually does not react with dilute acid.
Comparing reactivity fairly
  • The speed or violence of the reaction can be used to compare metal reactivity.
  • The temperature change can also be measured because more reactive metals usually release more heat.
  • For a fair test, each metal sample should have the same mass and surface area.
  • The same type and concentration of acid should be used each time.
Reactions with water
  • When a metal reacts with water, it forms a metal hydroxide and hydrogen gas.
  • General word equation: metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen.
  • Example: lithium + water → lithium hydroxide + hydrogen.
  • Only the most reactive metals react strongly with water.
  • Magnesium reacts only very slightly with water.
  • Zinc, iron, and copper do not react with water under these conditions.
Displacement reactions
  • A more reactive metal can displace a less reactive metal from its compound.
  • Example: magnesium + iron sulfate → magnesium sulfate + iron.
  • Magnesium displaces iron because magnesium is more reactive than iron.
  • Copper cannot displace iron from iron sulfate because copper is less reactive than iron.
  • If the added metal is less reactive than the metal in the compound, there is no reaction.
