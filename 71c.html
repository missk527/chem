Sustainable Development

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

1) Meaning of sustainable development
  • Sustainable development means meeting the needs of people today without harming the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
  • It is a way of developing human society and the economy responsibly over the long term.
  • This idea focuses on both present quality of life and future environmental protection.
2) Why sustainability matters
  • Unsustainable actions can use up Earth's limited resources.
  • They can also damage the environment and increase global warming.
  • These problems create negative effects for people who will live in the future.
3) Renewable and finite resources
  • One way to develop more sustainably is to use renewable resources instead of finite resources.
  • Wood is often more sustainable than plastic because more trees can be grown.
  • Plastic is usually made from crude oil, and crude oil is a limited resource.
4) Energy use in manufacturing
  • The sustainability of a product also depends on how much energy is needed to make it.
  • If a process uses a lot of energy from burning fossil fuels, it may not be very sustainable.
  • Low-energy manufacturing methods are usually better for the environment.
5) How chemistry can reduce environmental impact
  • Chemists can make manufacturing processes more efficient.
  • They can reduce the energy needed by using catalysts and choosing the optimum temperature and pressure.
  • They can also design processes that use smaller amounts of raw materials.
6) Reuse as the most sustainable option
  • After a product has been used, the most sustainable action is often to reuse it.
  • Reusing means using an item again for the same purpose or for a new purpose.
  • For example, a glass bottle can be refilled and used again.
7) Recycling glass
  • If a product is too damaged to reuse, it can often be recycled.
  • Glass is separated by color and type, then crushed and melted before being reshaped.
  • Recycled glass can become new bottles, jars, or glass wool for house insulation.
8) Recycling metals
  • Metals can also be recycled by melting and reforming them into new products.
  • Different metals usually need to be separated before recycling, such as copper and iron.
  • In some cases, mixed materials such as scrap steel can still be reused to reduce the need for newly extracted iron.
