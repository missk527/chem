Relative Formula Mass (Mr) & Percentage Composition

Video link: https://youtu.be/sNBvdRhEEps

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

1) Mass number vs relative atomic mass (Ar)
  • Mass number is found in the top-left of a nucleus symbol and equals the number of protons + neutrons in the nucleus.
  • Sometimes a nucleus symbol shows the relative atomic mass (Ar) instead. Ar is the weighted average mass of all isotopes of that element.
  • Example: chlorine atoms can have mass numbers 35 or 37, but chlorine’s Ar is 35.5 because it is the average based on isotope abundance.
2) Relative formula mass (Mr)
  • Relative formula mass (Mr) is the average mass of an entire compound.
  • To calculate Mr, add up the Ar values of every atom in the compound’s formula, using any numbers and brackets as multipliers.
3) Worked examples of Mr
  • Magnesium chloride (MgCl₂): Mr = Ar(Mg) + 2 × Ar(Cl) = 24 + 2 × 35.5 = 95.
  • Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄): Mr = 2 × 1 + 32 + 4 × 16 = 98.
4) Percentage mass of an element in a compound
  • Percentage mass of an element = (Ar of element × number of atoms of that element ÷ Mr of compound) × 100.
  • Example: %S in H₂SO₄ = (32 × 1 ÷ 98) × 100 = 32.7%.
5) Brackets matter: calcium hydroxide example
  • Calcium hydroxide is Ca(OH)₂. The subscript 2 multiplies both O and H inside the brackets.
  • Mr of Ca(OH)₂ = 40 + 2 × 16 + 2 × 1 = 74.
  • %O in Ca(OH)₂ = (2 × 16 ÷ 74) × 100 = 43.2%.
Quick formulas • Mᵣ = sum of (Aᵣ × number of atoms) • % mass of element = (Aᵣ(element) × atoms of element ÷ Mᵣ(compound)) × 100
