Notes (Key points from the speech)

What are anions?
  • Anions are negatively charged ions found in many salts and solutions.
  • We can identify specific anions using simple wet‑chemistry tests that produce a gas or a precipitate.
Test for carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻)
  • Add dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl) to the sample.
  • If carbonates are present: salt + CO₂(g) + H₂O are produced.
  • Confirm CO₂ by bubbling the gas through limewater (calcium hydroxide solution).
  • Positive result: limewater turns cloudy/milky.
Test for sulfate ions (SO₄²⁻)
  • Step 1 (acidify): add dilute HCl to remove carbonate and sulfite impurities that could give false precipitates.
  • Step 2: add barium chloride solution (BaCl₂) to supply Ba²⁺ ions.
  • If sulfate is present, barium sulfate (BaSO₄) forms.
  • Positive result: a white precipitate appears.
Test for halide ions (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻)
  • Step 1 (acidify): add dilute nitric acid (HNO₃) to remove carbonate/sulfite impurities.
  • Step 2: add silver nitrate solution (AgNO₃) to supply Ag⁺ ions.
  • A precipitate forms, and its colour identifies the halide:
  • Cl⁻ → AgCl: white precipitate
  • Br⁻ → AgBr: cream precipitate
  • I⁻ → AgI: yellow precipitate
Interpreting results and avoiding errors
  • Always acidify first to remove ions that could form misleading precipitates.
  • Cloudy limewater points to CO₂ and therefore carbonate.
  • White BaSO₄ precipitate indicates sulfate.
  • Silver halide colours distinguish chloride, bromide, and iodide.
