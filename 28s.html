Gas Volumes and Moles at RTP

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

1) Core gas volume equation
  • At room temperature and pressure, the volume of a gas can be found using volume = moles × 24.
  • The volume is measured in dm³ (decimetres cubed).
  • This rule works for any gas at RTP, so the gas type does not matter.
  • If the number of moles doubles, the gas volume also doubles.
Volume of gas at RTP volume (dm³) = moles × 24
2) Finding volume from moles
  • When the number of moles is given, multiply by 24 to get the gas volume.
  • Example: 3.5 mol of chlorine gas gives 3.5 × 24 = 84 dm³.
  • This is a direct one-step calculation.
3) Finding moles from volume
  • The equation can be rearranged to moles = volume ÷ 24.
  • Example: 60 dm³ of oxygen is 60 ÷ 24 = 2.5 mol.
  • Always keep the volume in dm³ when using this relationship.
Rearranged equation moles = volume ÷ 24
4) When mass is given first
  • If the question gives a mass instead of moles, first calculate moles using moles = mass ÷ Mr.
  • For water vapor, Mr(H₂O) = 18 because oxygen is 16 and two hydrogens add 2.
  • Example: 27 g of water vapor gives 27 ÷ 18 = 1.5 mol.
  • Then use the gas equation: 1.5 × 24 = 36 dm³.
Useful steps moles = mass ÷ Mr then volume = moles × 24
5) Using reacting gas volumes
  • In gas reaction questions, identify the limiting reactant if one reactant is in excess.
  • Example: in the ammonia equation, nitrogen reacts with excess hydrogen, so nitrogen controls the amount of product formed.
  • 18 dm³ of nitrogen gives 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 mol of nitrogen.
  • The molar ratio from N₂ : NH₃ = 1 : 2, so 0.75 mol nitrogen forms 1.5 mol ammonia.
  • The ammonia volume is 1.5 × 24 = 36 dm³.
6) Shortcut with gas volume ratios
  • If a question only converts between gas volumes, you can use the mole ratio directly.
  • Gas volumes follow the same ratio as gas moles at the same conditions.
  • Example: if the ratio between nitrogen and ammonia is 1 : 2, then 18 dm³ nitrogen makes 36 dm³ ammonia.
  • If nitrogen and hydrogen are in a 1 : 3 ratio, then 4 dm³ nitrogen reacts with 12 dm³ hydrogen.
7) Important condition
  • The number 24 only applies to gases at room temperature and pressure (RTP).
  • If temperature or pressure changes, gas volume changes too.
  • In exam questions, this 24 dm³ per mole rule is the standard one to use unless stated otherwise.
Final reminder 24 dm³ per mole only works at RTP.
