Carboxylic Acids

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

1) What carboxylic acids are
  • Carboxylic acids are a homologous series of organic compounds (like alkanes and alcohols).
  • They contain the functional group –COOH (the carboxyl group).
  • Their names end in –anoic acid.
Functional group: –COOH Naming: alkane name → replace “-e” with “-oic acid”
2) First members you should recognise
  • Methanoic acid (1 carbon)
  • Ethanoic acid (2 carbons)
  • Propanoic acid (3 carbons)
  • Butanoic acid (4 carbons)
  • When writing formulae, keep the –COOH group together at the end (e.g. CH3COOH).
3) Acid behaviour and weak ionisation
  • Carboxylic acids behave like acids, but they are weak acids.
  • They do not fully ionise in water, so only some molecules release H+.
  • Because ionisation is reversible, we show it with an equilibrium arrow.
General ionisation: RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺ (weak acid → partial ionisation)
4) Carboxylate ions and salts
  • The negative ion formed is called a carboxylate ion.
  • Its name ends in –anoate:
    ethanoic acid → ethanoate propanoic acid → propanoate
  • Example ionisation: propanoic acid forms propanoate ions and H+.
Example: CH3CH2COOH ⇌ CH3CH2COO⁻ + H⁺
5) Reaction with metal carbonates
  • Like other acids, a carboxylic acid reacts with a metal carbonate to form:
  • a salt (metal carboxylate) water carbon dioxide
  • Example: ethanoic acid + potassium carbonate → potassium ethanoate + water + CO2.
Acid + carbonate → salt + water + CO2
6) How carboxylic acids are made (oxidation of alcohols)
  • Carboxylic acids can be made by oxidising alcohols using an oxidising agent (adds oxygen / removes hydrogen).
  • The carbon chain length stays the same: oxidising an alcohol gives the carboxylic acid with the same number of carbons.
  • Example: butanol (4 carbons) oxidises to butanoic acid (4 carbons).
alcohol → (oxidation) → carboxylic acid e.g. butanol → butanoic acid
