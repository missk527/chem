pH Scale

Notes

  • pH measures how acidic or alkaline a solution is.
  • The pH scale runs from 0 to 14.
  • Low pH = acidic, high pH = alkaline, pH 7 = neutral.
  • Examples: stomach acid (~pH 2), acid rain (~pH 4), washing liquid (~pH 9), bleach (~pH 12).
  • Indicators are dyes that change color depending on pH.
  • Universal indicator shows a full range of colors across the pH scale.
  • pH probes measure pH electronically and give precise numerical values.
  • Acids release hydrogen ions (H⁺) in water and have pH less than 7.
  • Bases have pH greater than 7.
  • Alkalis are bases that dissolve in water and produce hydroxide ions (OH⁻).
  • Neutralisation happens when an acid reacts with a base to form salt and water.
  • Example: hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water.
  • H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O explains neutralisation at the particle level.
  • Common acids: hydrochloric acid, sulfuric acid, nitric acid.
  • Common bases: sodium hydroxide, calcium carbonate.
