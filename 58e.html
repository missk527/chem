Esters

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

What esters are
  • Esters are organic molecules with a characteristic functional group located within the carbon chain.
  • The functional group contains a carbonyl (C=O) next to an oxygen, commonly written as –COO–.
  • This group is often called the ester group or the ester link because it links the acid part to the alcohol part.
Typical properties
  • Many esters have pleasant smells, often described as sweet or fruity.
  • Esters are often volatile, meaning they evaporate easily.
  • Because of these properties, esters are commonly used in perfumes and food flavourings.
How esters are made (esterification)
  • Esters are made by reacting a carboxylic acid with an alcohol.
  • This reaction is called esterification.
  • An acid catalyst is used to speed up the reaction, commonly concentrated sulfuric acid.
Example: ethyl ethanoate
  • To make ethyl ethanoate, you react ethanoic acid with ethanol.
  • During the reaction, the acid provides an –OH group and the alcohol provides an H from its –OH group.
  • These combine to form water, which is the by-product of the reaction.
Key idea about the ester link
  • The ester link (–COO–) is the part of the molecule that connects the carboxylic acid fragment to the alcohol fragment.
