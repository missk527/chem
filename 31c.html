Atom Economy

Key Notes (rewritten from the speech)

1) Why atom economy matters
  • Commercial chemical reactions often produce more than one product.
  • Usually, only one main product is useful, while the others are treated as waste.
  • Atom economy helps companies measure how much of the reactants end up in the desired product.
2) Example reaction and useful product
  • In the reaction CO + H2O → H2 + CO2, the goal is to make hydrogen.
  • Here, carbon dioxide is not the target product, so it is considered waste.
  • The key question is: what percentage of the reactants becomes hydrogen?
3) Definition and formula of atom economy
  • Atom economy shows the percentage of reactant atoms that are incorporated into the desired product.
  • Atom economy = (Mr of desired product ÷ total Mr of reactants) × 100
  • If there is more than one useful product, you add the Mr values of all desired products.
4) Calculating atom economy for hydrogen production
  • For H2, the Mr is 2.
  • For the reactants: CO = 28 and H2O = 18, so the total Mr is 46.
  • Atom economy = (2 ÷ 46) × 100 = 4.35%.
5) Interpreting the result
  • An atom economy of 4.35% is very low.
  • It means only 4.35% of the reactants become the useful product.
  • The remaining 95.65% becomes waste, so the reaction is not very efficient in terms of material use.
6) High atom economy example
  • In the reaction N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3, the only product is ammonia.
  • Because there are no unwanted products, the atom economy is 100%.
  • All of the reactant atoms are used to make the desired product.
7) Why businesses care about atom economy
  • Raw materials cost money, so turning most of them into waste reduces profit.
  • Using lots of reactants to make a small amount of useful product is less sustainable.
  • Waste disposal can be expensive and can also harm the environment.
8) Improving industrial processes
  • One solution is to choose a more efficient reaction with a higher atom economy.
  • Another solution is to find a use for waste products so they become useful by-products.
  • Even low-value by-products are often better than paying to dispose of waste.
9) Other factors that affect profitability
  • Atom economy is important, but it is not the only factor in deciding whether a process is profitable.
  • Other factors include percentage yield, cost of raw materials, position of equilibrium, and rate of reaction.
  • Companies also consider the cost of maintaining conditions such as temperature and pressure.
