Naturally Occurring Polymers — Notes & Quiz Polypeptides · DNA · Carbohydrates

Video + exam‑focused notes + 40‑question practice

Notes (Key points from the speech)

What are naturally occurring polymers?
  • Naturally occurring polymers exist in nature and are not man‑made.
  • A polymer is a long chain made from many smaller molecules called monomers.
  • This video focuses on three examples: polypeptides, DNA, and carbohydrates.
Polypeptides and proteins
  • Monomers: amino acids.
  • Many amino acids join in a chain to form a polypeptide.
  • When a polypeptide folds (or multiple polypeptides combine), it forms a protein.
  • Different amino‑acid sequences produce many different proteins with different functions (e.g., enzymes and structural proteins).
Amino acid structure (key functional groups)
  • Every amino acid contains an amino group (–NH2) and a carboxylic acid group (–COOH).
  • These are attached to a central carbon.
  • The R group changes between amino acids and makes each amino acid different.
Condensation polymerisation in polypeptides
  • Adjacent amino acids join by a condensation reaction.
  • An OH from the carboxylic acid group and an H from the amino group combine to form water (H2O).
  • This creates a bond between the carbonyl carbon and nitrogen called an amide bond (also called an amide link or peptide bond).
  • When writing a general polymer equation, use n to show many repeating units form and nH2O is produced.
DNA as a polymer
  • Monomers: nucleotides.
  • Each nucleotide contains a base. There are four bases: A, T, G, and C.
  • Aside from the base, the rest of the nucleotide is the same, so there are four nucleotide types.
  • Different nucleotide orders create different codes called genes.
  • DNA has two polymer chains linked together to protect the code; it coils into a double helix.
Carbohydrates
  • Carbohydrates are made only of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen.
  • Monomers: monosaccharides (sugars) such as glucose and fructose.
  • Polymers: polysaccharides such as starch, cellulose, and glycogen.
  • By joining many sugar monomers (e.g., many glucose molecules), polysaccharides like starch can be formed.
Take-home message
  • Natural polymers are built from monomers by bonding reactions (often condensation).
  • Knowing the monomers, bonds formed, and examples helps you recognise polymer structures in exam questions.
