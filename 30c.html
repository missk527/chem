30c. Concentration in g per dm³

Key Notes (English)

Adapted from the provided speech. Focus: calculating concentration in grams per cubic decimetre, unit conversion, and using the formula triangle.

1) What dm³ Means
  • A cubic decimetre, written as dm³, is a unit of volume.
  • 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³.
  • 1 dm³ is also equal to 1 litre of water.
2) The Concentration Formula
  • Concentration is calculated using the formula concentration = mass ÷ volume.
  • Mass is measured in grams (g).
  • Volume must be measured in cubic decimetres (dm³).
  • The unit of concentration is g/dm³ or g dm⁻³.
3) Using the Formula Triangle
  • A formula triangle helps rearrange equations quickly.
  • The horizontal line means divide.
  • The vertical line means multiply.
  • If you cover C, you get mass ÷ volume.
  • If you cover m, you get concentration × volume.
  • If you cover V, you get mass ÷ concentration.
4) Example 1: Finding Concentration
  • If 40 g of sodium hydroxide is dissolved in 2.5 dm³ of water, use 40 ÷ 2.5.
  • The concentration is 16 g/dm³.
  • This can also be written as 16 g dm⁻³.
5) Example 2: Converting Units First
  • If 24 g of copper sulfate is dissolved in 480 cm³ of water, convert the volume first.
  • 480 cm³ ÷ 1000 = 0.48 dm³.
  • Then calculate 24 ÷ 0.48 = 50.
  • The concentration is 50 g/dm³.
6) Why Unit Checks Matter
  • Always check the units before you calculate.
  • Exam questions may try to confuse you by giving kilograms instead of grams or cm³ instead of dm³.
  • Using the wrong units leads to the wrong answer.
7) Finding Mass from Concentration and Volume
  • You can also use the formula to find mass.
  • Mass is calculated using mass = concentration × volume.
  • For a 400 cm³ sample with a concentration of 20 g/dm³, first convert 400 cm³ to 0.4 dm³.
  • Then calculate 20 × 0.4 = 8.
  • The mass of hydrogen chloride in the solution is 8 g.
