37C. Metal Extraction & Reduction

Key Notes (Synced with Video)

0:00 - 0:37 | Oxidation and Reduction
  • Oxidation means gaining oxygen.
  • Example: magnesium reacts with oxygen to form magnesium oxide.
  • Reduction means losing oxygen.
  • Breaking magnesium oxide into magnesium and oxygen is reduction.
0:37 - 1:18 | Metal Oxides in Nature
  • Most metals react with oxygen and form metal oxides.
  • Iron reacts with oxygen to produce rust.
  • Because oxygen is abundant, many metals are found as oxides.
  • Gold is very unreactive, so it is often found pure.
1:18 - 1:42 | Extracting Pure Metals
  • Most metals are bonded to oxygen in ores.
  • To obtain pure metals, the oxygen must be removed.
  • This process is called reduction.
1:42 - 2:12 | Reduction with Carbon
  • Carbon can remove oxygen from some metal oxides.
  • The oxygen combines with carbon to form carbon dioxide.
  • Example: copper oxide + carbon → copper + carbon dioxide.
  • Copper is reduced because it loses oxygen.
  • Carbon is oxidized because it gains oxygen.
2:12 - 2:48 | Reactivity Series
  • Only metals less reactive than carbon can be extracted using carbon reduction.
  • Carbon is placed in the reactivity series for comparison.
  • Zinc, iron, and copper can be extracted with carbon.
  • More reactive metals require electrolysis.
  • Electrolysis uses a large amount of energy and is expensive.
2:48 - 3:12 | Iron Ore Example
  • Ores are metal-rich compounds from which metals can be extracted.
  • A common iron ore is iron oxide (Fe₂O₃).
  • Iron oxide must be reduced to produce pure iron.
3:12 - 3:40 | Iron Extraction Equation
  • Iron is less reactive than carbon.
  • Therefore, iron can be extracted using carbon reduction.
  • Fe₂O₃ reacts with carbon when heated.
  • The products are iron and carbon dioxide.
